A skills-first tutor for the Jewish canon

The Jewish Learning Academy

Become someone who can open Jewish texts — a tutor that takes an adult from zero to reading the Jewish canon on their own, one move at a time.

The problem

Millions of Jewishly-curious adults feel locked out of the canon — no yeshiva, no day school, unsure how to even open a page. Every existing tool hands them content: translations, courses, references. None of them make a person able to read on their own.

The bet

Teach the transferable reading skills, not the content. Modeled on Math Academy's knowledge graph: one skill at a time, minimal cognitive load, always the single highest-leverage next move. The canon becomes the practice ground; the product is literacy itself.

What's built — and live today

  • Adaptive placement that pins a learner's frontier in ~7–15 self-checks — not one per skill — by inferring everything below what they can do.
  • A 55-skill knowledge graph of reading moves, from recognizing Hebrew letters to reading an unvocalized source independently.
  • Scaffolded lessons on real sources: introduce → practice → transfer to a different genre. The transfer is the skill.
  • Spaced review, targeted remediation, and a Hebrew-decoding on-ramp — the full 0→1 arc, alphabet to independent reading.

Where it honestly stands

A v0.1 prototype with a real engine. The learning machinery is built and working. What it needs next — and we are deliberately not faking — is (1) a live pilot to calibrate difficulty and validate the graph (running now), and (2) educator sign-off and authored assessment banks (the tooling to capture both is built). The graph grows on real pilot signal, not speculation.
