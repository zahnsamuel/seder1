# Learner pilot metrics

Seder’s first pilot measures learning behavior, not teacher ratings or contributor throughput.

## Core learner signals

- placement-to-first-session completion;
- percentage of learners who complete a canon phase checkpoint;
- independent-source accuracy after scaffolded practice;
- independent-source accuracy specifically after at least one completed course capstone, compared with scaffolded-source accuracy;
- number of capstones and canon bridges completed before an unseen-source encounter;
- review return rate and recovery after an uncertain answer;
- number of distinct source contexts per established skill;
- time to the first successful Gemara argument map;
- learner-reported clarity, challenge, and sense of progress.

## Interpretation

Low accuracy alone is not failure: it can signal an appropriately difficult source. The key diagnostic is whether a learner improves after feedback and retrieval, and whether the next unfamiliar source remains navigable.

Do not interpret a completed capstone as full mastery by itself. The useful signal is a sequence: a learner completes a source connection, succeeds or repairs an unseen-source encounter, and later retrieves the same move in another setting.

## Privacy

Use aggregate pilot reporting. Keep learner names and source-response records private; avoid collecting sensitive personal or religious-practice data that is not needed for learning.
