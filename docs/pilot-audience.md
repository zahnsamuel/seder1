# Seder pilot audience

## Primary learner

Independent adults ages 18–35 who want to enter the Jewish canon for the first time. They may be intellectually serious and highly motivated, but they should not be assumed to have Jewish day-school background, Hebrew vocabulary, family practice, synagogue fluency, or a study partner.

## Product implications

- Explain Jewish terms at first encounter without condescension.
- Keep primary text visible; translation is available on demand, never a hidden prerequisite.
- Make the next action unmistakable after every answer.
- Teach the social and textual conventions of a Daf explicitly: Mishnah, Gemara, baraita, verse, objection, distinction, Rashi, and later practice.
- Treat unfamiliarity as normal. The product should convey moral seriousness and invitation, not insider status.
- Provide independent-reading checkpoints, but do not punish a learner for using translation, hints, or review.

## Pilot success signal

After several sessions, a learner should be able to open an unfamiliar short source, locate the active question, name the function of the next line, use translation strategically, and explain why they want to continue.
