# Gemara canon strategy

Berakhot is the flagship first arc, not the whole Gemara product. Seder’s Gemara curriculum should use a sequence of gateway tractates that expose learners to different kinds of Talmudic reasoning and Jewish life.

## Stage 1 — universal reading tools

Hebrew/Aramaic signals, Mishnah grammar, Daf navigation, source maps, question/answer/proof, objection/distinction, baraita, verse, and independent short reading.

## Stage 2 — gateway tractates

| Tractate | Why it belongs early | Core learning payoff |
|---|---|---|
| Berakhot | Prayer, Shema, blessings, the opening of Shas | Enter a sugya; Torah → Mishnah → Gemara |
| Shabbat | Cases, domains, categories, action | Read a counted Mishnah and legal distinctions |
| Eruvin | Space, measures, communal boundary | Follow purpose and limit in a legal structure |
| Pesachim | Time, ritual action, ambiguous language | Separate action, time, tool, and interpretation |
| Sukkah | Physical structure, reason, dispute | Read measure, invalidity, and proof |
| Bava Metzia | Claims, evidence, fairness | Follow a civil-law dispute and response |

## Stage 3 — reading across Shas

Every later tractate should inherit the same source-map and language tools, while adding its own vocabulary, institutions, themes, and argument types. The goal is not completing pages quickly; it is progressively needing less scaffolding.

## Quality standard

Each tractate arc needs: an entry orientation; 3–5 connected sugyot; a vocabulary bank; varied retrieval in different source contexts; a source-map activity; and an independent checkpoint. No tractate should be represented only by a decorative card or one isolated lab.
